# Machine test brief — AI-Native Athlete Management System (USI · AMS)

Source: "KTSPL - Machine Test PM.pdf" (Khelo Tech, APM role). Condensed faithfully.

## Objective
Design and prototype an AI-native Athlete Management System for a modern sports ecosystem. NOT static screens.
Demonstrate: product thinking, systems thinking, workflow understanding, operational depth, AI integration, enterprise-grade UX architecture.
Must feel like a real SaaS platform used by federations, Olympic programs, academies, high-performance centres, sports science teams.
Support: athlete lifecycle management, sports science workflows, medical systems, performance tracking, analytics, AI-driven operations.

## Prototype expectations
Production-grade feel, enterprise SaaS quality, realistic workflows, operationally dense, NOT generic dashboard templates.
Interactive workflow coverage (static mockups insufficient): clickable flows, transitions, operational actions, drill-down interactions, detail drawers, state changes, workflow progression.
AI layer: AI recommendations, predictive alerts, workflow automation, operational copilots, anomaly detection, natural-language operations, AI-generated summaries, predictive injury warnings, automated insights.

## Mandatory modules
1. Dashboard / Command Center — executive overview, operational KPIs, athlete readiness, participation analytics, injury alerts, AI recommendations, drill-down analytics. Must demonstrate hierarchy filtering, role-based visibility, real-time operational thinking.
2. Athlete Management — athlete registry, athlete profile, enrollment workflow, document management, tagging systems, verification workflows, readiness scoring, athlete detail panels. Mandatory workflows: athlete onboarding, athlete approval flow, profile completion, coach assignment.
3. Training & Periodisation — macro/meso/micro cycle planning, session builder, exercise library, workload tracking, attendance systems, RPE collection, ACWR calculations. Must demonstrate operational training flow, session assignment, coach workflow.
4. Medical & Injury Intelligence — PRIORITY MODULE. Interactive body map, injury reporting, rehab workflows, return-to-play progression, injury timelines, medical notes, wellness monitoring. The body map MUST be interactive and operational: clickable anatomy regions, injury states, overlays, severity mapping, rehab interactions. NOT acceptable: static illustrations, flat diagrams, generic medical graphics.
5. Sports Science — readiness monitoring, fatigue analytics, wearable integrations, GPS analytics, recovery tracking, workload visualisation, AI-based risk detection.
6. Nutrition — diet plans, hydration tracking, supplement management, compliance tracking, body composition trends.
7. Assessments & TID — testing systems, benchmarks, talent scoring, progression analysis, field testing workflows, comparative analytics.
8. Analytics & BI — federation-level analytics, drill-down hierarchy, predictive analytics, operational insights, exports/reporting, KPI intelligence.
9. AI Copilot — AI operations assistant, contextual recommendations, predictive insights, workflow suggestions, natural language interaction layer.

## Evaluation weights
Product thinking 20% · Workflow depth 20% · Systems thinking 15% · UX quality 15% · AI-native thinking 15% · Interaction design 10% · Visual quality 5%.
NOT evaluating: visual polish alone, Dribbble-style dashboards, generic admin panels.
ARE evaluating: operational depth, enterprise workflow understanding, scalable architecture, realistic SaaS thinking, sports-tech understanding, systems integration thinking, AI-first product strategy.
Preferred: component-driven design, scalable layouts, modular systems, workflow-oriented UX, operational logic. Bonus: React prototypes, motion, design systems, state logic, realistic interaction modelling.
Personas: athletes, coaches, sports scientists, physiotherapists, nutritionists, federation admins, performance directors, operations teams.
Bonus points: exceptional systems thinking, advanced interaction modelling, AI workflow innovation, realistic operational flows, strong medical/injury intelligence systems, scalable architecture thinking, mobile-first operational workflows, production-grade prototype quality.
